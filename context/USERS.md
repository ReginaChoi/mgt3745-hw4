# User research and jobs

## Interview synthesis
INT-03 is a recent accounting graduate and the closest participant I could get to my target user. INT-01 and INT-02 are professionals in judgment-heavy roles, retained as indirect evidence about the work students are preparing for.

### INT-01
Participant code / relevant circumstance / date: INT-01 / In-person interview about the participant's work as a Finance Manager at an auto dealership / 9/2/2026

What happened and the participant's workaround: He walked me through his daily contract review: checking paperwork for missing details, negotiating loan terms, advising sales staff. No formal workaround, just habits, double-checking documents himself.

What was reported, what you observed, and what you infer: He reported that one missing contract detail can cause real delays, so checking happens constantly. What surprised me was that coworkers rely on his judgment too, not just customers.

Evidence that confirms, challenges, or leaves the framing uncertain: Supports HW1's idea that judgment-heavy work differs from routine data entry, but he never mentioned AI.

### INT-02
Participant code / relevant circumstance / date: INT-02 / Online interview about a recent excess and obsolete inventory process / 9/8/2026

What happened and the participant's workaround: He described processing around 1,300 excess-and-obsolete lots a month: get the list, locate each lot, separate and weigh parts, remove them from Oracle. He keeps a physical paper list to track found lots, despite Excel, EasyM, and Oracle being available.

What was reported, what you observed, and what you infer: The hardest part isn't data entry, it's physically finding misplaced lots, and none of his digital tools solve that.

Evidence that confirms, challenges, or leaves the framing uncertain: Shows even well-equipped workers fall back on simple tracking when tools don't fit the real problem. Says nothing about accounting or AI.

### INT-03
Participant code / relevant circumstance / date: INT-03 / Interview with a 2025 accounting graduate (managerial and financial accounting) about her transition from student to job seeker / 9/15/2026

What happened and the participant's workaround: She wanted a career in audit and is currently unemployed. She described having no method for figuring out which skills to develop and said she was never exposed to real-world examples or internships. She did not track her skills and said she had no internship experience coming out of school to point to when applying for jobs. There was no workaround; she simply didn't do it.

What was reported, what you observed, and what you infer: Three things surprised me. First, she said she never thought about tracking skills at all, so the gap isn't a bad tool, it's that the activity never occurred to her. Second, when I asked whether AI changed how she prepared or how she saw accounting jobs, she said no, flatly, which cuts against the premise I started from in HW1. Third, the gap she actually named was experience, not skills: in hindsight she should have gotten at least one internship, because she left school with none. She also said coming out of Georgia Tech was not as useful in the job search as she expected.

Evidence that confirms, challenges, or leaves the framing uncertain: This confirms the skill-clarity half of my framing and challenges the rest. Confirms: she never figured out which skills her target career needed, and named "seeing relevant skills" as the part of the proposed tool she'd find most useful. Challenges: she never tracked skills and never wanted to, so F-02 is not a Must-be for this segment the way INT-02 made it look. Challenges harder: AI was irrelevant to her preparation, so the AI-anxiety framing carried from HW1 is not supported by the one target-adjacent person I've actually asked. Her interest in the tool was conditional and lukewarm ("Yes, because hopefully I'd get a job"), which I'm treating as weak support, not validation.

## Two job statements
JOB-01: When I am deciding what to focus on as an accounting student, I want to know which skills the career I'm aiming at actually requires, so I can build toward it instead of guessing.
Evidence: known, INT-03 said she never figured out which skills to develop because she was never exposed to real-world examples or internships, and named seeing relevant skills as the most useful part of the proposed tool.

JOB-02: When I'm applying for my first accounting job, I want concrete experience I can point to, so I'm not graduating with nothing to show an employer.
Evidence: known, INT-03 said in hindsight she should have gotten at least one internship because she had no experience coming out of school. Note that this job is only partly served by my spec: a tracking tool can record experience but cannot create it.

## Two user profiles
PROFILE-01: Accounting student or recent graduate preparing for a first accounting role.
Relevant role (known): Studied managerial and financial accounting, graduated 2025, wanted a career in audit, currently unemployed (INT-03).
Circumstances (known): Had no method for identifying which skills her target career required, and no exposure to real-world examples or internships while in school (INT-03).
Needs (known): Wants to see which skills a chosen accounting path actually requires; named this the most useful part of the proposed tool (INT-03). Wants concrete experience to show employers (INT-03).
Constraints (known): Never tracked skills and never considered doing so, so a tracking tool addresses a need she did not feel while in school (INT-03). AI did not affect her preparation or her view of accounting jobs, so the AI framing behind this project is not supported by this interview.
Constraints (assumed): One graduate is not a population. Whether current students share her experience is still untested.
Evidence IDs: INT-03 (direct); HW1 framing (assumed).

PROFILE-02: Inventory Specialist.
Relevant role (known): Manages daily cycle counts and monthly E&O processing of roughly 1,300 lots.
Circumstances (known): E&O work is added on top of his full daily workload, not a replacement for it.
Needs (known): A reliable way to track which lots he's already located.
Constraints (known): Existing digital tools don't solve this problem, which is why he still uses paper.
Evidence IDs: INT-02.
