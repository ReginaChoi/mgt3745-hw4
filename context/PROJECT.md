## Candidate Problem

I am concerned about how artificial intelligence is going to affect the field of accounting as well as future job opportunities for newly graduated students like myself. A few friends and church members have suggested that I should change my major because they think AI will take over accounting jobs, and hearing this repeatedly from people I trust has made me question how accounting students can actually prepare for a future that is so uncertain.

## Wickedness Test

*Hard to formulate a problem statement: Since AI will affect many different aspects of accounting, and nobody knows for sure what exactly is going to happen in the future in terms of development of the field, it is not easy to formulate a single concise problem statement.

Solutions are good or bad, not true or false: Solutions, such as learning AI and data analytics, having a minor in a related area, or concentrating on areas that require a lot of judgment in accounting, are not right or wrong in absolute terms. They can fit very well in one person's career plan, but not in someone else's, depending on what position or company the student works for.

No immediate or ultimate test: One cannot be sure today if a student's preparation is working or not. Today, a student can prepare themselves for using AI tools, but it might happen that the skills employers value will be different in five or ten years. We won't know for some time if this approach was good or not.

May be a symptom of another problem: The problem in question might have nothing to do with AI per se. These might be bigger trends that are changing, and they would change anyway regardless of AI, only much faster with the use of AI.*

**Verdict: This is a wicked problem.**

## Three Framings

**Framing A (the obvious one):** How can I, as an accounting major with a Fintech minor, make sure the entry-level bookkeeping and data-entry skills I am currently learning do not become outdated by the time I graduate and start applying for jobs?

**Framing B (stakeholder shift):** What skills should public accounting firms and government agencies like the IRS actually be looking for in new graduates, if routine bookkeeping tasks are increasingly automated?

**Framing C (goal shift):** How can I build toward a long-term goal like forensic accounting or a role at the IRS, where judgment and investigation matter more than routine data entry, so that my career is not defined by whichever entry-level tasks AI happens to automate first?

## Commit and Justify

**Chosen framing: Framing C**

**Why:** For me, Framing C makes more sense because my goal is not simply to avoid AI taking over accounting jobs in general, it is to build toward a specific career in forensic accounting or a role at the IRS, where judgment, investigation, and interpreting numbers matter more than routine data entry. Students cannot fully control how technology changes the workplace, but I can control which direction I grow in. This framing does not assume that switching majors or avoiding AI is the right response. Instead, it focuses on developing toward a role where the skills AI is least likely to replace, judgment, ethics, and investigation, are the ones that matter most.

**What this framing makes invisible:** This framing will likely make it hard to see that certain accounting jobs, such as basic bookkeeping roles, will be made outdated rather than altered. Because it focuses on my own long-term goal, it may not address broader problems, such as employers cutting entry-level positions across the industry or universities changing their accounting curriculum. In addition, it places most of the responsibility on me to grow toward a specific role, which means the responsibility of firms, professional associations, or certification bodies in defining what an accounting career actually requires may be overlooked. Finally, because forensic accounting and government roles like the IRS are still a specific and somewhat narrow path, this framing may not be as useful for accounting students who do not share that same goal.

## Oath Check

**The tool I already assumed:** Initially, I thought that the use of AI technologies like generative AI would be one of the solutions to the problem, along with training accounting students in technical and AI skills.

**What would have to be true for it to be justified:** For this tool to be justified, accounting students would actually need AI and technical skills to succeed in the careers they want. Employers would also have to value these skills, and learning them would have to help students become more adaptable rather than simply giving them unnecessary skills to deal with. I should not assume that AI education is the answer just because AI is part of the problem.

## Adequacy Sketch

Good enough for now means completing one informational interview with someone in a judgment-heavy accounting-adjacent role by a set deadline, and being able to point to evidence of active development in at least three judgment-heavy skills (for example, a specific class assignment, a case competition, or a documented practice case), rather than only being able to name the skills in the abstract. I would know this point has been reached when I can produce that evidence on request, the same way forensic accounting requires evidence rather than an assertion that "controls are adequate." An unmeasurable version of this sketch, such as "feeling prepared," would not satisfy it; a dated interview held and three skills with attached proof would.
